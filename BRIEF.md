# Aphelion Run

Product brief. This is the current description of the game. Discussion continues from here.

## 1. Executive summary

- **Logline:** A cross-platform, asynchronous space strategy game where players navigate a live, moving solar system to contest a dwindling network of solar harvesting satellites — either to secure corporate profits or deliver a stolen lifeline to Earth's slums.
- **Genre:** Asynchronous space strategy, with economy and fleet management.
- **Platform:** The same game on a wide PC screen and a tall phone screen.
- **Setting:** 700 years in the future. Earth after a collapse, and the inner solar system.

## 2. World and factions

Humanity has a much smaller presence on Earth than it once did. Wars and disease have ravaged the globe, leaving a stark wealth disparity between the pristine corporate paradise domes and the polluted, forgotten slums known as the Ashlands.

Humanity has recently achieved near-light-speed travel and massive energy storage. It is nowhere near technology that could enclose the Sun. Harvesting energy directly from the Sun is a relatively new, highly volatile industry. Humans cannot, and do not want to, live in orbit around the Sun.

The legal energy corporation refuses to provide ships or liability insurance to its workers. Every pilot in both factions is an independent contractor risking their own capital.

- **Sol Systems, Inc. (SSI):** A ruthless corporate conglomerate with a legal monopoly on solar harvesting. They price energy so high that only the well-off can afford it. Their pilots are corporate contractors in heavily armored, high-defense cargo frigates.
- **Ashland Marauders:** A loose network of pirates, rogue pilots, and freedom fighters out of Earth's slums. They steal energy to power black-market medical bays, weapon factories, and air scrubbers in the Ashlands. They fly jury-rigged, maneuverable ships built for speed and electronic warfare.

## 3. Core gameplay loop

The game is played entirely in space. Each player has one home base and one ship. The home base orbits a planet or a moon. The Sun has harvesting satellites and no bases.

The ship runs three routes. It fills at the Sun and unloads into the home warehouse. It hauls energy from that warehouse to an Earth station and brings the cash home to the bank. It spends that cash at another body, on the materials the home does not yield, and hauls those materials home. Every one of those burns is paid in energy taken from the home warehouse, so a pilot who wants materials still has to keep going to the Sun.

Home → Sun → home warehouse. Home warehouse → Earth station → home bank. Home → a body that yields what the ship lacks → home warehouse.

A surplus of energy is worth more as cash when that pilot's price on Earth is high. The cash buys a larger load of materials than a trade between two ships can move. The load still has to be flown home, and the energy spent flying it is gone.

### Home base

The home base is the energy warehouse, the repair bay, and the bank. Improvements are built there, and only there. It can orbit any planet or moon. It cannot orbit the Sun.

A base on a body that yields a material receives that body's material steadily, free, into the warehouse. It receives no other body's. A moon and a planet work the same way: the home is subject to that body's yield. The pilot has plenty of the home yield and travels for every other one. No yield stands in for another, so every pilot goes to every yielding body except the one they live on. The Sun and Earth are the only exceptions. The Sun is where energy is mined. Earth is where energy is sold. The giant planets yield a material of their own, scooped from orbit, and their gravity still makes a close pass cheaper when they sit in the right part of their orbit.

No faction owns a material. Two pilots of either faction can base on the same body, and each receives its yield. The free stream is not the stock visitors buy.

Where the home sits is a choice about distance. A direct burn costs less stored energy between closer orbits, so Mercury is the cheap hop to the Sun and the Moon is the cheap hop to Earth's market. The Moon is not a combat zone. Venus sits between them. A base from Mars outward pays more for the same direct burn. The inner bases stay ahead on a direct burn. They still visit every yielding body. The planet that can bend the course this month is what makes a given trip affordable, and that planet changes as the orbits move.

### Materials

Five parts of the ship. Every yielding body feeds one of them, and every step up in strength spends every body's yield. The free yield at home is only that body's share. A neighbor's yield is not included, and a planet's yield is not included in its moons. This brief sets no prices and no amounts.

The four giant planets have no ground to mine. Each one still has a material, invented from what that planet is already thought to hold, and changed so a ship can carry it.

- **Hydrogen metal** comes from Jupiter. Under the clouds, hydrogen is pressed into a metal. In this setting the scoops bring it up and it stays solid. Hull, armor, and guns spend it, along with the yields of Mercury, Mars, Phobos, and Deimos.
- **Helium rain** comes from Saturn. Helium falls through the sky in droplets. Siphon modules spend it, along with the yields of the Moon, Ganymede, and Proteus.
- **Mantle ice** comes from Uranus. The hot ice under the clouds is lifted out and kept. Shields spend it, along with the yields of Europa, Callisto, Mimas, Enceladus, Tethys, Dione, Rhea, Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, Oberon, and Nereid.
- **Storm tar** comes from Neptune. Heat and wind draw carbon out of the methane. Electronic warfare and the light fittings spend it, along with the yields of Titan, Iapetus, and Triton.
- **Energy tanks** spend the yields of Venus and Io.

A pilot who lives on Europa still docks at Callisto, at Mimas, and at every other body on that list. The same holds for every home and every other body.

### Buying and trading

Cash buys materials at a base on a body that yields them. The ship has to dock there. Cash does not buy materials at home, and it does not buy them at Earth. The Earth station pays cash for energy. It does not sell materials, and it does not buy them.

Each yielding body keeps a stock for sale, separate from the free stream its residents receive. The stock is finite and refills. One purchase cannot clear it for good.

Materials go aboard the ship. They have weight. A foreign base does not hand over energy. It will not sell a load that leaves the ship short of the energy to depart for home on the wide path today, so the ship can always leave. If the return grows more expensive after the purchase, the ship waits in that orbit until the alignment cheapens it. That orbit is not a combat zone.

Two pilots docked at the same base can trade materials between their ships. A base does not pay cash for materials. Cash comes from energy sold at Earth, or from cash taken off a beaten ship.

### Launch and orbital alignment

The board is a top-down map of the solar system. Planets orbit the Sun at realistic relative speeds, so the path from a home base to the Sun, to Earth, and out to any other body changes every day. So does which planet can bend that path.

- The player reads the current alignment and plots a trajectory.
- Once a ship is sent, it moves along that line on the map.

### Delta-v

Delta-v is the change in speed a route demands. The ship pays it by burning stored energy. That energy is the same store it hauls and sells, so a burn spends cargo.

These runs are orbital transfers. The planets already move at tens of kilometers per second, and a contractor's burn is a change on that order. Spending the cargo to cross the system near the speed of light would burn the profit, so these haulers do not fly that way.

A ship cannot depart unless the energy aboard covers the route it is about to fly. The cost depends on the orbit it is in, the orbit it is going to, how much energy it is carrying, and whether a planet on the path can bend the course. The cost of a direct transfer is set by the two orbits, which is why an inner base stays cheaper. Where the planets sit decides whether that direct path is open today, and whether a third body can cheapen it.

Cash has no weight. The drive still needs energy to leave. The Earth station buys only the surplus above the burn home. A ship that arrives with nothing above that reserve can leave, and it has nothing to sell.

- **Stuck:** Below the reserve, the ship stays in its current orbit. At the Sun, if the satellite still holds energy, the ship keeps siphoning until it can leave. If the satellite is empty, the ship stays idle and can be attacked until the alignment makes the departure cheap enough. At home, the ship waits until the warehouse can cover the departure, or until the alignment makes it cheaper. At a foreign base, the ship was not sold a load it cannot lift, and it waits out a bad alignment there.
- **Heavier costs more:** The same change in speed takes more energy when the ship is carrying more energy or more materials. Past a point the cargo is so heavy that what is left cannot buy the trip. Siphoning stops before that point even if the satellite still holds energy. That is why a drained satellite is not always a full ship.
- **Assist:** A gravity assist is the efficient burn. The ship is already moving at orbital speed, and a close pass lets a planet's gravity change that speed, so the engines spend less stored energy to reach the next orbit. Any planet can be that pass. It works only when the planet is in the part of its orbit that bends the ship toward the destination. A course can use more than one planet. Venus and the other inner planets bend courses toward the Sun and across the inner system. Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune bend courses outward, including the course in to that planet's own moons. A pilot bound the other way uses the same pass. A close pass in the wrong month saves nothing. A wide path stays clear of the planet, spends the full reserve, and leaves the hull alone. A close path on a good alignment spends less energy, arrives sooner, and wears the shields and hull down.
- **Recall:** A defeated ship is still sent home to the repair bay. The victor takes the energy and the cash that were aboard. The recall does not deliver a cargo.

### Outer orbit and inner orbit

At the Sun, space is split in two:

- **Outer orbit:** A stable staging area. The player can scan the inner orbit and see which satellites are occupied, and by whom.
- **Inner orbit:** The low zone beside the Sun, where the harvesting satellites are.

### The Earth sale

Earth orbit works the way the Sun's does. The outer orbit is the approach. The inner orbit holds the faction energy stations. A player sells only at their own faction's station. The transfer takes time. If the ship is defeated before the transfer finishes, the cargo is still energy, and the victor takes it. When the transfer finishes, the station pays cash onto the ship for the surplus above the burn home and undocks it into the inner orbit. The station does not store energy, and it does not bank cash.

That cash stays on the ship until the ship reaches its home base and banks it. Until then, any opposing ship in Earth's inner or outer orbit can attack, including a ship parked at an Earth home. A defeat sends the loser home to repair and gives the victor whatever unbanked cash or energy was aboard. Stolen cash is not banked either. The thief has to carry it to their own home. Leaving Earth orbit is what ends the hunt. Orbits of other planets and moons are not combat zones.

Banking at an Earth home is the shortest cash haul. The ship can still be attacked the whole time it remains in Earth orbit, including after it is home and after a repair return. That is why a contractor lives beside Earth, on the Moon, rather than in Earth's orbit.

### Satellites

Each satellite holds a finite pool of solar energy. When it is drained, it must recharge from the Sun, and players move on to another.

Ships fight with their stats. The player does not steer the shots. A battle can finish while the player is away.

- **Harvest:** Dock at an empty or allied satellite and siphon until it is empty, or until another load would drop the ship under the reserve for today's departure. When the siphon stops, the ship is idle in the inner orbit with its haul aboard, and it can be attacked. A departure plotted from the terminal is flown when the siphon stops. The ship stays exposed while it siphons and until it leaves the inner orbit. A ship with no departure plotted stays on the empty satellite.
- **Attack:** Drop onto a satellite held by the other faction, or onto a ship idle there. If the attacker wins, they take the energy and the cash that were aboard, plus scrap from the wreck. The defender is sent home to the repair bay. The recall does not deliver a cargo.

## 4. Payload penalty

Combat power falls as cargo weight rises, so a full hauler is weaker than an empty ship.

Active combat power = hull strength + component bonuses + crew skills − payload weight.

Energy has weight. Materials have weight. Cash does not. The same weight raises the energy a burn consumes.

- **Light:** A ship carrying little saleable energy, and few materials, is fast and fires quickly. The run out to the Sun, and the run home from Earth with cash, are light. The drive still keeps a reserve for the burn. With an empty tank, the ship cannot depart. The cash ship fights at full strength, and it is carrying the whole payout.
- **Heavy:** Siphoned energy makes the ship heavy. So does a hold full of materials. It turns slowly and its guns cycle slowly, on the way home to the warehouse, on the way to an Earth station, and on the way home from a purchase. The player has to leave the Sun while the leftover energy can still pay for the trip.

## 5. Repair and recovery

Ships are never permanently destroyed.

- A defeated ship is sent back to the player's home base and locked in the repair bay. If that home is in Earth orbit, the ship returns to a combat zone.
- Repairs and improvements are made at the home bay. They spend the materials that part is built from. The free stream covers the home element's share. The other shares wait on what the ship has brought back. Missing a material delays that work. It does not by itself keep the ship from flying.
- Repair time depends on the ship. A big cargo frigate takes longer than a light interceptor.
- A damaged ship that won, or survived, can be sent home early to repair before the next run.

## 6. The market

Only a completed transfer at an Earth station changes the price of energy on Earth. Energy sitting in a home warehouse does not.

- A player sells at their own faction's station. SSI deliveries move the legal price. Marauder deliveries move the black-market price.
- If SSI contractors deliver a flood of energy, the legal price drops and corporate payouts fall.
- When the legal price drops, the domes buy up the surplus and the slums go without power. Black-market demand spikes.
- Stolen energy then pays more, so more players run as Marauders. Legal supply falls, the legal price rises, and the cycle starts again.
- A fight that steals energy before the transfer finishes is not a delivery, so it does not move the price.
- The station pays cash onto the ship. That cash is banked only at the home base. Banked cash is spent at other bodies, on the materials they yield. A pilot spends it to buy a larger improvement than the free stream and a trade can cover. Materials are not bought or sold at Earth. This brief sets no prices and no amounts.
- Players hold energy in the home warehouse and sell when their side's price is high. That high price is the premium. Selling into a flood is how the premium disappears.

## 7. Screens

- **Phone, tall layout:** A corporate or hacker terminal. Check flights, set sell orders, manage repairs, plot the ship's next path, including the departure from a satellite. The terminal shows what a plotted path costs in energy with today's alignment, whether the ship has enough aboard to depart, what the warehouse holds, and whether a purchase at the destination still leaves enough energy to come home.
- **PC, wide layout:** The same data, plus a 3D playback of the automated battles so the player can see how their ship, shields, and crew actually performed.
